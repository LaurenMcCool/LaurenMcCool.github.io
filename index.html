ABOUT ME

WHO AM I?

Hi, I’m Lauren McCool, a third-year Bachelor of Education (Secondary) and Bachelor of Arts (Design Innovation and Technology) student at ACU.

I’ve always had a love for all things creative and hands-on, and I’m passionate about bringing that energy into the classroom. My specialisations are Textiles and Industrial Technologies, which allow me to explore both the artistic and practical sides of design.

What excites me most about teaching is helping students discover their own creativity, whether that’s through problem-solving, making, or experimenting with new ideas. I believe in creating learning spaces where students feel supported to take risks, express themselves, and develop real-world skills they can carry beyond the classroom.

PHILOSOPHY

THE HUMAN NEED TO CREATE

At the heart of every human there is the desire to create, to make, build and design. This instinct drives curiosity and innovation and are the foundations of craftsmanship, problem solving, and innovation. TAS subjects provide one of the richest outlets for this creativity and help develop qualities that are essential to our future industries and communities.

THE ART OF LEARNING

Learning is not just about absorbing knowledge, it’s about exploring, experimenting, and finding meaning in mistakes.

As a teacher, I see my role as more than just delivering curriculum, it’s about fostering young minds and nurturing the next generation of thinkers, makers, and innovators.

My teaching philosophy centres on meeting each student’s unique learning needs, balancing structure with flexibility with hopes of maintaining clear expectations and routines, while also helping students understand how they learn and how to manage their emotions. Every student deserves to feel valued, capable, and inspired in the classroom.

CREATING A POSITIVE LEARNING ENVIRONMENT

A positive classroom is the foundation for meaningful learning. I want my classroom to be a space where students feel safe, supported, and inspired to grow.

When students feel comfortable, they are more willing to take risks, collaborate, and persist through challenges. I believe that learning happens best when students feel seen and respected when they know their voice matters.

PORTFOLIO

I have inter-disiplinary experience that allows me to cater to a variety of different classroom environments.

With experience across all areas of TAS, my main subject specialisations is Textiles and Design with a minor in Industrial Technologies, focusing on both timber and metalwork.

As a creator myself I am deeply inspired by biophilic design with the idea that our environments and creations should connect us back to nature. I feel that designing and creating with nature in mind encourages sustainability, wellbeing, and mindfulness and as a future educator I would like to encourage my students to develop deeper understanding of how design can nurture harmony between people and the planet. Creating with ties to nature reminds us that innovation and sustainability can coexist ans that thoughtful design can both inspire and sustain life.

I love the balance of precision and creativity that timber work requires; it teaches patience, problem-solving, and respect for the material itself. Metal Gallery

Metalwork is a discipline I’ve recently begun exploring, and it has quickly become a rewarding part of my creative practice. I’m fascinated by how strength and precision can combine to produce designs that are both functional and expressive. Working with metal has challenged me to think critically about technique, accuracy, and design intent, while still leaving space for creativity and experimentation. As I continue to develop my skills, I’m excited to bring this learning into the classroom — encouraging students to approach metalwork with curiosity, persistence, and pride in craftsmanship.
